Korva · a ring organ for a tiny transformer

Two tiny transformers, trained, each seeing only the last 12 ticks. One also gets a ring organ: a comb of 52 delay rings (laps 13 to 64 ticks) that carries the past forward and tells attention which lap the rhythm repeats on. Rhythms here repeat every 20 to 60 ticks, longer than the window. Watch what each model expects next.

What training showed

  • The organ supplies the rhythm. Without it no model learned the 20–60 tick rhythms in the same training (0.00). The 64-tick transformer reached 0.20 with four times the training; the GRU stayed at 0.
  • Attention supplies what is local. The organ alone cannot predict fills (0.10); with attention on top they stay at 0.95. After a pattern change the pair also recovers better than the organ alone (0.55 against 0.42).
  • The lap choice does the work. Handed three random laps instead of the three best-tuned ones, the same model falls to 0.13.
  • Gating the writes made no difference here (0.91 either way). The organ's slow surprise matters for choosing laps, not for protecting the memory.

The organ

Every tick, each ring compares the 8 bands with what it held one lap ago (the comparator), adds the mismatches to a slow surprise that fades over about 32 ticks, and rewrites its slot only while surprise is high. The three rings with the lowest surprise hand attention three tokens: what they expect next, what just mismatched, their surprise and their lap. Nothing in the organ is learned.

The transformers

Two layers, two heads, width 48, RoPE, attention over the last 12 ticks only. Same size, same training, same data. The organ model's attention can also read the three organ tokens at every layer. The weights on this page are the trained ones, run tick by tick in your browser.

Limits

Synthetic training rhythms only, so tapped rhythms and real audio are outside what the models saw and work less well. The organ's lap selection is hand-made (lowest surprise wins). The comparison is small-budget: bigger baselines trained for longer would likely learn more of the rhythm than they did here. Nothing here is new to the field; it shows what this organ does for a tiny model.
